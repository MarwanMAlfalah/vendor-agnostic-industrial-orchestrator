# Conclusion Draft

This paper presented a deterministic prototype for governance-aware, vendor-agnostic orchestration of industrial workflows. The prototype separates planning, capability matching, governance evaluation, and adapter-mediated execution, and it supports heterogeneous mock interfaces representing OPC UA, REST, and legacy systems. The evaluation shows that policy-centric metrics are essential: baselines that remove governance or policy filtering can execute workflows while producing unsafe or policy-bypassing behavior.

The aligned journal evaluation provides stronger empirical evidence than the initial canonical case set. Across 240 deterministic scenarios, `reference_v1` and `langgraph_runtime` achieve aligned policy compliance of 1.0, final outcome accuracy of 1.0, unsafe execution of 0.0, approval bypass of 0.0, and trace completeness of 1.0. The LangGraph runtime further demonstrates controlled approval continuation, graph-native tracing, and checkpoint-based restart recovery in local prototype experiments.

The contribution is intentionally bounded. The prototype uses deterministic synthetic scenarios and mock adapters; it does not claim certified safety, production deployment, or live PLC integration. Future work should evaluate the approach with richer industrial datasets, stricter schema validation, live or hardware-in-the-loop adapters, operator-in-the-loop approval studies, and more detailed standards mapping. Within its stated scope, the work provides a reproducible foundation for policy-aware industrial orchestration research and a stronger basis for an IJTech-oriented journal extension.
