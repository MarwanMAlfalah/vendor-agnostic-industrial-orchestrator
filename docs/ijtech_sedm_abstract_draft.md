# Abstract Draft

Industrial operations often depend on heterogeneous vendor interfaces, including OPC UA endpoints, REST services, and legacy systems. This heterogeneity complicates supervisory orchestration when workflows must respect governance constraints such as approval gates, blocked actions, and safe-abort behavior. This paper presents a deterministic research prototype for vendor-agnostic, governance-aware industrial orchestration. The prototype separates planning, capability matching, governance evaluation, and adapter-mediated execution, and is evaluated against deterministic baselines that remove governance or architectural stages. An optional LangGraph runtime is also studied for controlled post-approval continuation, graph-native traces, and checkpoint-based recovery.

The empirical evaluation combines canonical governance cases, mixed multi-step workflows, post-approval continuation tests, a same-process checkpoint reopen/resume demonstration, and a scaled deterministic benchmark. The scaled benchmark includes a 240-scenario journal profile and a 500-scenario extended profile covering workflow length, governance pattern, adapter mix, approval condition, and containment condition. Because runtime capabilities differ, aligned metrics report both original scenario labels and runtime-specific expected outcomes. In the aligned journal profile, `reference_v1` and `langgraph_runtime` both achieve policy compliance of 1.0, final outcome accuracy of 1.0, unsafe execution of 0.0, and trace completeness of 1.0. Governance-ablated baselines remain substantially weaker, with conditional unsafe execution reaching 1.0 for direct execution and no-policy-filtering baselines.

The results provide prototype-level evidence that policy-centric evaluation is essential for industrial orchestration research. The study does not claim certified safety, production deployment, or live PLC integration; all scenarios are deterministic and use mock adapters.
